# Changelog

## Unreleased
- Realtime Beauty's glass reflects and transmits an area light through its emitter's outline sampled from the emitting side: the outline's samples take the emission normal (`matrix · -Z`), as the diffuse lobes do. The glass path passed the light's `+Z` axis, which clipped every sample and zeroed a glass reflection of an area light facing its receiver (`render/scene.rs` `glass_direct_contribution`).
- Realtime Beauty transmission follows EEVEE 5.2.2's default glass closures (`gpu/shaders/material/gpu_shader_material_principled.glsl`, `MAT_REFRACTION`; `draw/engines/eevee/shaders/eevee_nodetree_lib.bsl.hh`, `bsdf_lut`; `eevee_closure.bsl.hh`, `bxdf_lightprobe_ray`; `eevee_thickness_lib.bsl.hh`; `eevee_deferred_combine.bsl.hh`). A Principled Transmission Weight and a Glass BSDF take their reflectance and transmittance from EEVEE's split-sum LUT (`eevee_lut.cc`: `brdf_ggx`, `btdf_ggx` for IOR above one and `bsdf_ggx` for IOR below one, embedded as `render/eevee_transmission_tables.f32le` by `scripts/extract_eevee_transmission_tables.py`), weighted by `(1 - metallic) · transmission`, with EEVEE's multiscatter normalisation. The transmitted colour is `T · √base` for a Principled or the Glass Color. The scene's world is sampled at the mirror direction and at the refracted probe direction, each blurred by its perceived roughness, and each sample is first truncated to the probe's `UFLOAT_11_11_10` capture format (six mantissa bits for red and green, five for blue), which is the coloured-world deficit EEVEE shows at world colours below one. The thickness is the Material Output's Thickness socket when linked, and otherwise the smallest world-space bounds dimension (`eevee_shader.cc`). A thickness of any size gives two refraction events: the transmitted colour is squared, and the probe direction crosses the sphere model, or the slab model for `Material.thickness_mode` Slab (`material.create`/`material.update` accept `thickness_mode`, and the `.blend` bridge reads and writes it). The diffuse base and the dielectric specular layer take `(1 - transmission)`, so a glass surface no longer adds its base colour as diffuse light. Measured on Blender 5.2.2 with a white world of 1: a white glass sphere's centre is 0.9668 in EEVEE and 0.9674 in the model, and IOR 0.8 glass is 0.9873 in EEVEE against 0.9878. `tests/realtime_glass_transmission.rs` compares the clear, tinted and rough spheres of `fixtures/native_oracle/eevee_glass_transmission.py` pixel by pixel to 2.5e-3, and `tests/realtime_glass_world.rs` does the same over a coloured, elevation-dependent Environment Texture (`eevee_glass_world.py`) to 1.25e-2, which separates a wrong probe direction (0.035 to 0.050) from the capture truncation (0.0105). Direct light through glass is EEVEE's LTC light pass, not the path tracer's single-event GGX glass lobe: each light's emitter (a point or spot light's disk, or its sphere when Soft Falloff is off; a sun's disk; a rectangle or an ellipse) is integrated through the GGX matrices of EEVEE's utility table (`eevee_ltc_lib.bsl.hh`, `eevee_light_lib.bsl.hh` `light_ltc`, `eevee_bxdf_microfacet.bsl.hh` `bxdf_ggx_light_transmission`), with `scripts/extract_eevee_transmission_tables.py` extracting `ltc_mat_ggx` and `ltc_disk_integral`. The reflection takes the glass reflectance; the transmission takes its colour squared with a thickness (two events). A light is attenuated by EEVEE's facing and influence (`light_attenuation_facing`; `light_influence_attenuation` with the default `eevee.light_threshold` of 0.01), and a transmission is shadowed by the thickness-amended ray (`shadow_eval`): its origin moves through the object by the thickness toward the light, and is biased along `reflect(Ng, L)`. The shadow ray is traced against the scene, where EEVEE's is a shadow map, so the two differ wherever the map's depth bias and texel size decide. `tests/realtime_glass_direct.rs` renders two glass spheres under a point light and an area light, above and below (a black world), and compares them with EEVEE's pixels to 1.6e-2; the Python mirror `fixtures/native_oracle/eevee_glass_direct_mirror.py` reproduces EEVEE to 8.0e-3 over the same pixels (the rim of a narrow transmitted lobe). Losses, reported rather than rendered: ray-traced transmission (`use_raytrace_refraction`, off by default in EEVEE) is not implemented; the Thickness socket is read for materials with a node graph only (a native `Material` has none); Every realtime direct light fades with EEVEE's light influence (`light_influence_attenuation`, `attenuation_radius_get`): a local light's radius is `sqrt(power / threshold)`, where the threshold is the scene's `eevee.light_threshold` (imported and exported, default 0.01), or its `cutoff_distance` when `use_custom_distance` is on (both imported and exported). The fade multiplies every closure of the light. A spot's cone is EEVEE's `light_spot_attenuation`, stretched by the light's object scale; the Cycles cone ignored the scale and misses EEVEE by 0.80 at a Z scale of 2. A sun has the disc of its `sun_angle` (imported and exported, default 0.526°, `tan(angle / 2)` as EEVEE's `shape_radius`): its shadow averages sixteen rays over the disc, where the penumbra was a fixed 0.003 rad, and its glass light uses the disc's LTC. `tests/realtime_direct_light_cases.rs` renders seven cases of a Lambert plane under one light and compares every pixel with EEVEE's: the influence cut-off to 2.5e-3, the spot to 5e-3, and the sun's penumbra to 0.1 at its edge (mean 1e-3); the Python mirror `fixtures/native_oracle/eevee_direct_light_mirror.py` reproduces EEVEE to 2.2e-3 for the spot and 1.2e-3 for the cut-off. A light's closure multipliers `diffuse_factor`, `specular_factor` and `transmission_factor` (imported and exported) scale its direct light per closure class, as EEVEE's `power[LIGHT_DIFFUSE]`, `power[LIGHT_SPECULAR]` and `power[LIGHT_TRANSMISSION]` do (`eevee_light.cc`, `shape_parameters_set`): the diffuse class is the diffuse and sheen lobes, the specular class the specular and coat lobes and the glass reflection, and the transmission class the glass transmission. The influence radius takes the larger of the diffuse and specular factors, as EEVEE's does. The path tracer does not apply them, as Cycles does not (the Cycles source at Blender v5.2.2 has no reference to `diff_fac`, `spec_fac`, `transmission_fac` or `volume_fac`). A sun's disc is integrated for its specular and coat lobes over the disc's sixteen Vogel-spiral directions, each weighted by its own shadow ray, so a glossy highlight takes the disc's shape; its diffuse and sheen lobes take the centre's value times the disc's mean visibility, which is the disc integral of a flat lobe. `tests/realtime_light_factors.rs` checks the factors as ratios of Potter's own renders, and the sun's disc effect against EEVEE's (the disc-to-delta ratio of a glossy plane, EEVEE 0.817 and Potter's sampled 0.811 in the mirror, `fixtures/native_oracle/eevee_light_factors_mirror.py`). `volume_factor` scales a light's realtime volume in-scatter, as EEVEE's `power[LIGHT_VOLUME]` does, and the in-scatter fades with the light's volume influence radius (`light_attenuation_volume`: `sqrt(volume_factor · power / threshold)`, or the custom distance). The path tracer applies neither, as Cycles applies neither (no reference to `volume_fac` in `intern/cycles` at v5.2.2). `tests/realtime_volume_factor.rs` renders a pure scatterer at factors 1, 0 and 0.5: EEVEE's half-factor render is half its base to 7.0e-4 (its sampling noise), its zero-factor render is black, and Potter's zero-factor render remains black to 1e-6 while its half-factor response is within the same native-measured 1.5e-3 bound. Loss: the realtime volume in-scatter uses Cycles' light radiance per sample, where EEVEE's volume light uses `point_radiance_get` (for area lights a mean-representative-point scaling, and for a sun 1), so the in-scatter's magnitude is not EEVEE's for those lights. Realtime world-probe reads now follow the capture format for every closure: glossy and glass radiance is truncated to `UFLOAT_11_11_10` (a world of 0.6 reads 0.59375), and diffuse irradiance is truncated just below the radiance, so a unit world reads 0.9921875 in a white diffuse, as EEVEE's spherical-harmonic capture does (`eevee_transmission::irradiance_probe_radiance`, measured at world values 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1 and 2). This moves realtime diffuse under worlds other than 0 or a power of two by up to 0.8 per cent; no realtime oracle was re-measured in this change. The lobes and the environment take their view as the camera ray's reverse, as EEVEE's `world_incident_vector` does: an orthographic camera's view is its ray direction, where they took the vector to the camera's position, which is wrong for a parallel ray (a perspective camera is unchanged).
- Realtime Beauty's filtered samples retain the environment-only ambient-occlusion rule; two-sided glass uses a view-facing normal and a non-degenerate LTC frame at parallel and antiparallel views, and direct glass reflection obeys a light's `visible_glossy` flag.
- Realtime sun and area shadow rays use a first-hit BVH traversal when the scene has no transparent casters; transparent-shadow and point/spot paths keep their existing traversal.
- CI keeps all-feature tests optimized with debug assertions while omitting DWARF from test binaries.
- The glossy blur (Cycles' `bsdf_microfacet_blur`, `kernel/closure/bsdf_microfacet.h`) raises only the microfacet alpha. The multiscatter energy and dielectric darkening stay at the closure's setup roughness, as Cycles' `energy_scale` is set once at setup; `native_glossy_blur` pins a roughness-0 metal's Filter Glossy 1.0 darkening against Blender 5.2.2.
- Bump nodes follow Cycles 5.2.2 in the path tracer (`kernel/svm/displace.h`, `svm_node_set_bump`): the screen differentials `dP/dx` and `dP/dy` come from each path's footprint (`kernel/camera/camera.h`, `kernel/geom/shader_data.h`, `kernel/util/differential.h`): the camera's compact `dP` and `dD` per raster pixel, grown along the path as `dP + t·dD`, and carried through each pass-through (a light, a medium boundary and a transparent pass) and each surface bounce. A subsurface exit carries the entry's footprint with `dD` zero (`kernel/integrator/subsurface.h`, `subsurface_bounce`). After a non-transparent bounce `dD` widens to `scale · sqrt(avg roughness²)` when that is larger (`kernel/closure/bsdf.h`, `bsdf_widen_dD`), where the average weights each closure's squared roughness by `pdf(wo) · sample_weight` at the direction the path leaves along, and a sampled singular closure weighs `1e6` (`kernel/integrator/surface_shader.h`, `_surface_shader_bsdf_eval_mis`). The scale is `min(1, filter_glossy)` (`scene/integrator.cpp`). Filter Glossy is Blender's `cycles.blur_glossy` (0 to 10, default 1.0), imported from `.blend` and set with `render.update`. It also blurs the path's microfacet closures: after a bounce whose sampled pdf is below `1 / filter_glossy` (the smallest on the path, `min_ray_pdf`), the specular, coat and glass roughness becomes `sqrt(max(sqrt(1 - blur_pdf) / 2, roughness²))` (`kernel/integrator/surface_shader.h`, `surface_shader_prepare_closures`; `kernel/closure/bsdf_microfacet.h`, `bsdf_microfacet_blur`). The height is sampled one Filter Width along each differential, and the normal is `fw·|det|·N − scale·sign(det)·surfgrad`. The Bump node's Distance defaults to Blender 5.2.2's 0.001, and Filter Width (default 0.1) is read. An unlinked Bump Normal is the shading normal (`kernel/svm/displace.h`), and the shader Math node gains `FRACT` (`kernel/svm/math_util.h`). Verified against Blender 5.2.2 renders in `fixtures/native_oracle/bump/`: `tests/native_bump_slope.rs` (a sawtooth of two-pixel teeth whose mean depends on the footprint: Blender's red mean is 0.079 at Filter Width 0.1 and 0.256 at 1.0), and `tests/native_bump_filter_glossy.rs` (a metallic floor of roughness 0.3 reflecting a sawtooth emitter that light sampling does not reach, rendered by Blender at Filter Glossy 1.0 and 0.0, which differ by 15.7 and 40 times the tolerance in red and green; Potter is checked at both). The glossy blur has no oracle yet. Known gaps: the realtime engine, the raster preview and the baking pass have no camera footprint, so their bump keeps the symmetric finite difference over a fixed step, and Filter Width does not apply there; the panoramic, fisheye and depth-of-field cameras use the same pinhole finite difference of their rays, not Cycles' per-projection differentials; the volume scatter keeps `dD` unwidened (Cycles' `volume_phase_widen_dD`); shadow rays do not widen `dD` (`shade_surface.h`); and a subsurface entry neither blurs nor lowers `min_ray_pdf` (Cycles' BSSRDF sample).
- Ocean modifier now evaluates Blender 5.2.2's seeded spectra (Phillips, Pierson-Moskowitz, JONSWAP, Texel-Marsen-Arsloe), FFT displacement with choppiness, foam and spray corner color layers (including inverted spray), and preview/render resolutions through the shared evaluation purpose. `bake --kind ocean` writes half-float OpenEXR caches per channel and frame with a SHA-256 manifest, and records them on the modifier as `baked_ocean_cache`; cached frames take precedence over live evaluation. Edits to the init-update properties (frame range, spectrum, seed, resolution, wind, and the others Blender marks) or a choppiness zero crossing invalidate the cache, as Blender does.

An NLA stack over `time` starts from the property's Blender default (1.0, `DNA_modifier_types.h`), not the stored value, as `animsys` does (`anim_sys.cc`, `nla_blend_value`, `nla_combine_value`, `nladata_flush_channels`): COMBINE adds the strip's offset from that default, and a channel that an enabled track binds is written back as the default when no strip covers the frame. Verified by `tests/fixtures/ocean_parity/generate_nla_time.py` (Blender 5.2.2) through `ocean_time_nla_matches_blender_522_oracle`. Other modifier-parameter curves are preserved with a `blender.animation.modifier_parameter` loss and are never evaluated.
- Baked Ocean EXR caches store their rows as Blender's EXR writer does (top row first, `openexr_api.cpp`), and reads reverse them back to the bottom-up buffer. `bake` and every later read resolve a cache `filepath` through one rule: `//` and relative paths are relative to the project, so a bake run from another directory writes where the modifier reads.
- `EVALUATION_ENGINE` is now `potter-eval-5`, so preview and pick artifacts recorded before this change are not reused.
- The FLOAT Boolean solver's triangle overlap and candidate trees, and its connect-islands tree, clamp their epsilon to `FLT_EPSILON` as Blender's `BLI_bvhtree_new` does (`BLI_kdopbvh.cc`). Before, a `double_threshold` of 0 inflated those boxes by nothing; now they are inflated by `FLT_EPSILON`, so a FLOAT result can change where the threshold is at or near 0. The `threshold_zero_touching` row of `tests/blend_boolean_float_regressions.rs` pins this against Blender.
- The path tracer renders row bands on worker threads. Each worker records its shading warnings in its own sink, and the sinks are merged in pixel order, so the warnings and the pixels match the serial render. Ray traversal reads leaf-ordered triangle data and tests each child box once per visit. The object's inverse matrix is computed once per object, shader link lookups use a per-material index, and the camera frame is built once per eye per render. A shadow ray and the camera's medium probe read only a hit's media and shadow transparency, so they no longer evaluate the displacement gradient's bump samples. A shader warning that only those samples can record is therefore no longer recorded from a shadow ray, and an error that only those samples raise no longer fails a shadow ray; path and camera hits still record and raise both. The world is evaluated only where a path uses it. On the perf fixture set the decoded pixels and warnings match origin/main; no fixture records a warning or error from bump samples alone, so that case is not covered by the comparison.
- The path tracer's pixel sampling follows Cycles 5.2.2's film pixel filter, importance-sampled as Cycles does (`intern/cycles/scene/film.cpp` `filter_table`, `util/math_cdf.cpp` `util_cdf_invert`, `kernel/camera/camera.h` `camera_sample`, `kernel/util/lookup_table.h`). `RenderSettings` holds `pixel_filter_type` (`BOX`, `GAUSSIAN`, `BLACKMAN_HARRIS`, default `BLACKMAN_HARRIS`) and `filter_width` (default 1.5, Blender's RNA range 0.01 to 10). A `.blend` imports and exports `scene.cycles.pixel_filter_type` and `filter_width`. Behaviour change: scenes without these keys, including Potter-native projects, now render with Blackman-Harris 1.5 instead of a one-pixel box, so edges are softer.
- Scene schema 8: a node's Blender object visibility is only `ray_visibility` now. The version 7 copy of the `visible_*` flags in `blender_object_metadata.rna_properties` moves into it on load (`model/migrate.rs` `upgrade_v7`), from Blender's default for the node's kind, so an old project renders and exports the flags Blender had. A node whose flags match the default keeps none. `RayVisibility` is a mask with accessors, and its serialised form is unchanged (six named flags).
- Blender object ray visibility (`visible_camera`, `visible_diffuse`, `visible_glossy`, `visible_transmission`, `visible_volume_scatter`, `visible_shadow`) is imported, stored on the node as `ray_visibility` (omitted when Blender's default: lights are hidden from camera rays), exported, and honoured as Cycles does (`PATH_RAY_*`, `integrator/path_state.h`). The path tracer masks each object per ray kind in closest-hit and shadow queries, and each volume object per ray kind in its volume traversal. A camera ray meets a light whose camera visibility is on, and its emitter shows in the image (`integrator/shade_light.h`, `lights_intersect`). A BSDF ray is `Diffuse`, `Glossy` or `Transmission` by its lobe, a total internal reflection is `Glossy`, a volume-scatter ray is `VolumeScatter`, and a shadow ray is `Shadow`. A BSDF ray that meets a light excluding its kind still ends the segment and spends the transparent budget, but emits nothing (`integrator/shade_light.h`, `integrate_light_forward`). A surface's direct lighting drops the lobes that the light excludes (`surface_shader.h`, `_surface_shader_exclude`), keeping the full BSDF density in the MIS weight. A volume scatter light-samples only a light that admits volume scatter (`SHADER_EXCLUDE_SCATTER`). Before, a non-default flag was dropped on `.blend` import. Realtime Beauty honours mesh `visible_camera` and `visible_shadow`, and light diffuse and glossy, as EEVEE does. It warns `UNSUPPORTED_FEATURE` for a light's camera, transmission and volume-scatter flags, which EEVEE honours (`eevee_light_shape_display`, `eevee_light.cc`) and realtime does not. A light's `visible_shadow` is stored and round-trips silently, as neither Cycles nor EEVEE reads it. glTF, USD, OBJ, PLY, and STL exports report `object.ray_visibility` losses for non-default flags; FBX and Alembic refuse them through the same loss gate. `node.update` accepts `set.ray_visibility`, and an edit wins over the imported RNA copy on export.

Their RGB radiance uses EEVEE's jittered, tile-sampled cumulative volume transmittance (`eevee_light_shape_display.bsl.hh`, `eevee_volume_lib.bsl.hh`); committed macOS captures cover the emitters and absorber at tile sizes 8 and 1, and an overlap regression checks differently colored lights sum.

The 96 × 192 test quadrature overshoots the narrow lobe at roughness 0.12 and below (+0.10% at 0.12, +0.17% at 0.10), so those oracles need the finer grid.
- A committed oracle compares a rough metal sphere (roughness 0.3) under a point, a rectangle, and a sun light, under a world alone, and under all of them together, at four bounces against Blender 5.2.2 Cycles (`tests/fixtures/native_oracle/metal_sphere_lights.py`, `tests/native_metal_sphere_lights.rs`). Its tolerance is the 99.9% t bound of the combined seed standard errors. It measures per-light normalisation and the world, with no rendered-output change.
- Materials carry Cycles' `use_bump_map_correction` (`cycles.use_bump_map_correction`, on by default; `intern/cycles/blender/addon/properties.py`). The `.blend` bridge imports and exports it, the material schema and set operation accept it, and the material inspection reports it. Off, the specular, coat and glass normals are not lifted above the geometric normal, and the diffuse and sheen lobes take no grazing smoothing, as Cycles' `SD_USE_BUMP_MAP_CORRECTION` gates `maybe_ensure_valid_specular_reflection` and `bump_shadowing_term` (`kernel/closure/bsdf_util.h`, `kernel/closure/bsdf.h`).
- A voxel volume's Volume output keeps one phase lobe per closure, each with its own anisotropy and its share of the scatter, as Cycles mixes the closures' phases (`kernel/integrator/volume_shader.h`). Its scatter events pick a lobe by the mixture density. Before, a voxel volume took the heaviest closure's anisotropy for all its scatter.
- Breaking: `shader::evaluate_surface` returns a `SurfaceShading` (`closures`, `volume`, `displacement_height`) instead of one `BsdfParams`. A Mix Shader or Add Shader keeps its arms as closures, as Cycles does (`intern/cycles/kernel/closure/alloc.h`), and no parameter is blended: `BsdfParams::mix` and `BsdfParams::add` are removed. A Mix weights the first arm `1 - Fac` and the second `Fac`; an Add concatenates both arms at weight 1 each. Each closure keeps its own normal, roughness, coat, alpha and transparent tint, so an Add with an opaque arm keeps the transparent share of its other arm. The realtime raster sums its closures by weight, and the path tracer samples one closure per bounce by its weight, with multiple importance sampling across the closure pdfs, as Cycles does (`intern/cycles/kernel/integrator/surface_shader.h`). Surface closures are not merged; volume closures are summed by weight into the medium (`intern/cycles/kernel/integrator/volume_shader.h`). The displacement height is measured along the hit's geometry normal, as Cycles' bump from displacement does (`scene/shader_graph.cpp`). glTF keeps its existing loss and fallback, unchanged: a Mix or Add of two surfaces reports `material.shader_graph.unrepresentable_surface` and keeps one arm, because a textured colour cannot be averaged symbolically (`exchange/material_surface.rs` is not changed by this entry).
- Path-traced surfaces whose shading normal differs from the geometric normal follow Cycles 5.2.2 in four places. A shadow ray's start follows Cycles' `shadow_ray_offset` (`kernel/light/sample.h`): the geometric normal is flipped to the transmission side when the light lies behind the smooth shading normal, so a rough glass lit from behind is not blocked by its own surface, and a light below the geometric surface is blocked by it. A reflection sample below the geometric normal is rejected when drawn (`kernel/closure/bsdf_diffuse.h`, `bsdf_microfacet.h`). A closure whose normal is bent off the smooth normal is zero at evaluation when the light lies on the other side of the smooth surface, except the sampled closure, which Cycles evaluates with `is_eval = false` (`kernel/closure/bsdf.h`, `bump_shadowing_term`); its diffuse and sheen lobes take Conty's GGX shadowing term at grazing light. The specular, coat and glass lobes take their normal raised so their reflection stays above the geometric normal (`maybe_ensure_valid_specular_reflection`, `kernel/closure/bsdf_util.h`), which Cycles applies by default (`use_bump_map_correction`); the diffuse lobe keeps the shading normal. Bump from displacement sets the smooth normal the shading-side test measures against, as Cycles' `sd->N` does. The realtime raster is unchanged. On the closure-list oracle, a Principled arm with a tilted normal moves from about 0.0046 to 0.0013 against the Cycles reference, within a bound of 0.0036.
- Principled subsurface scattering follows Cycles 5.2.2 for its four methods: Burley, random walk (the default), random walk skin and random walk legacy (`kernel/closure/bssrdf.h`: `bssrdf_setup_radius`, `bssrdf_setup`, `bssrdf_burley_eval`, `bssrdf_burley_pdf`, `bssrdf_burley_root_find`, `bssrdf_burley_sample`, `bssrdf_sample`, `bssrdf_dipole_compute_Rd`, `bssrdf_dipole_compute_alpha_prime`; `kernel/integrator/subsurface_disk.h`, `subsurface_disk`; `kernel/integrator/subsurface_random_walk.h`, `subsurface_random_walk`, `subsurface_random_walk_remap`, `subsurface_random_walk_coefficients`; `kernel/integrator/subsurface.h`, `subsurface_bounce`, `subsurface_entry_bounce`, `subsurface_scatter`, `subsurface_shader_data_setup`; `kernel/svm/closure.h` for the closure weights). A Subsurface Weight above zero takes that share of the diffuse closure, and a subsurface closure of weight `base × weight × diffuse layer` enters the surface with probability proportional to its weight. Burley's path leaves at a point that a disk probe along one of the surface's three axes finds on the same object: at most four hits are kept by Cycles' reservoir (`subsurface_disk.h`, `scene_intersect_local`), importance resampled among them, with the weight multiplied by `num_hits / 4` when more were seen. The random walk methods refract the view direction through a GGX interface of the surface's roughness squared (`subsurface_entry_bounce`), walk free flights through the object with Dwivedi-guided and opposite-interface sampling and channel MIS, and end at the object's surface, which is shaded as the diffuse exit of weight one. The walk starts exactly at the surface point and does not hit the triangle it entered through on its first bounce (`subsurface_bounce`, `ray.self.prim`). The random walk albedo is the d'Eon mapping from the base colour (`subsurface_random_walk_remap`); the legacy and skin closures use d'Eon's legacy fit, and van de Hulst's mapping when the anisotropy is negative. The skin's entry is a cosine lobe into the surface half the time and otherwise a refraction through an interface of roughness one, its radius is scaled by `sqrt(3 (1 - alpha'))` with the dipole albedo `alpha'` of the base colour under its IOR (`bssrdf_setup_radius`), and its lobe is not weighted by its probability, as in Cycles. Burley's and the legacy radius is the Principled radius times scale times `0.25 / π`; the random walk's is unchanged. Blender's defaults are radius `(1, 0.2, 0.1)` and scale 0.005, which Potter had as 0.05 and as `(1, 1, 1)`. A channel whose raw radius is below Cycles' `BSSRDF_MIN_RADIUS` falls back to diffuse for every method, and a Burley closure after a diffuse bounce falls back to diffuse, as Cycles does; the random walk keeps its closure. Thin Wall replaces the BSSRDF with Cycles' thin branch (`svm/closure.h`) for every method: no probe or walk, and the diffuse keeps `1 - Subsurface Weight` plus the thin reflection `Subsurface Weight × sat(0.5 (1 - Subsurface Anisotropy))`. Its translucent transmission (`bsdf_thin_subsurface_setup`) is not evaluated, so Thin Wall is reported as `UNSUPPORTED_FEATURE` under `shader.principled.thin_wall`. The Principled's `subsurface_method` node property selects the method (`BURLEY`, `RANDOM_WALK`, `RANDOM_WALK_SKIN`, `RANDOM_WALK_LEGACY`). A Mix or Add of two arms under different methods keeps each arm's closure with its own method, as Cycles keeps its closures separate. Subsurface IOR is read by the skin method and Subsurface Anisotropy by the random walk methods and the thin branch. The raster preview and the realtime engine still shade the diffuse lobe without subsurface scattering (a known gap). Verified by `tests/native_subsurface_sphere.rs` against Blender 5.2.2 renders of a sphere under a point and a disk light (`subsurface_sphere.py`, rendered live). At subsurface scale 6 the Burley case differs from no subsurface by 0.040 in red, and the random walk case differs from Burley by 0.119, so an absent or misscaled closure of any method is detected.
