# Changelog

## Unreleased
- Ocean modifier now evaluates Blender 5.2.2's seeded spectra (Phillips, Pierson-Moskowitz, JONSWAP, Texel-Marsen-Arsloe), FFT displacement with choppiness, foam and spray corner color layers (including inverted spray), and preview/render resolutions through the shared evaluation purpose. `bake --kind ocean` writes half-float OpenEXR caches per channel and frame with a SHA-256 manifest, and records them on the modifier as `baked_ocean_cache`; cached frames take precedence over live evaluation. Edits to the init-update properties (frame range, spectrum, seed, resolution, wind, and the others Blender marks) or a choppiness zero crossing invalidate the cache, as Blender does.
- Ocean `time` follows ID-bound Action and NLA curves per live frame and per bake frame, through the existing Action/NLA frame, influence, and blend rules. The Blender bridge binds only Ocean `time` (matched by the actual `Modifier.path_from_id("time")`) to the Object's active or NLA ActionSlot. Other modifier-parameter curves are preserved with a `blender.animation.modifier_parameter` loss and are never evaluated.
- `EVALUATION_ENGINE` is now `potter-eval-5`, so preview and pick artifacts recorded before this change are not reused.
- Principled subsurface scattering follows Cycles 5.2.2 for its four methods: Burley, random walk (the default), random walk skin and random walk legacy (`kernel/closure/bssrdf.h`: `bssrdf_setup_radius`, `bssrdf_setup`, `bssrdf_burley_eval`, `bssrdf_burley_pdf`, `bssrdf_burley_root_find`, `bssrdf_burley_sample`, `bssrdf_sample`, `bssrdf_dipole_compute_Rd`, `bssrdf_dipole_compute_alpha_prime`; `kernel/integrator/subsurface_disk.h`, `subsurface_disk`; `kernel/integrator/subsurface_random_walk.h`, `subsurface_random_walk`, `subsurface_random_walk_remap`, `subsurface_random_walk_coefficients`; `kernel/integrator/subsurface.h`, `subsurface_bounce`, `subsurface_entry_bounce`, `subsurface_scatter`, `subsurface_shader_data_setup`; `kernel/svm/closure.h` for the closure weights). A Subsurface Weight above zero takes that share of the diffuse closure, and a subsurface closure of weight `base × weight × diffuse layer` enters the surface with probability proportional to its weight. Burley's path leaves at a point that a disk probe along one of the surface's three axes finds on the same object: at most four hits are kept by Cycles' reservoir (`subsurface_disk.h`, `scene_intersect_local`), importance resampled among them, with the weight multiplied by `num_hits / 4` when more were seen. The random walk methods refract the view direction through a GGX interface of the surface's roughness squared (`subsurface_entry_bounce`), walk free flights through the object with Dwivedi-guided and opposite-interface sampling and channel MIS, and end at the object's surface, which is shaded as the diffuse exit of weight one. The walk starts exactly at the surface point and does not hit the triangle it entered through on its first bounce (`subsurface_bounce`, `ray.self.prim`). The random walk albedo is the d'Eon mapping from the base colour (`subsurface_random_walk_remap`); the legacy and skin closures use d'Eon's legacy fit, and van de Hulst's mapping when the anisotropy is negative. The skin's entry is a cosine lobe into the surface half the time and otherwise a refraction through an interface of roughness one, its radius is scaled by `sqrt(3 (1 - alpha'))` with the dipole albedo `alpha'` of the base colour under its IOR (`bssrdf_setup_radius`), and its lobe is not weighted by its probability, as in Cycles. Burley's and the legacy radius is the Principled radius times scale times `0.25 / π`; the random walk's is unchanged. Blender's defaults are radius `(1, 0.2, 0.1)` and scale 0.005, which Potter had as 0.05 and as `(1, 1, 1)`. A channel whose raw radius is below Cycles' `BSSRDF_MIN_RADIUS` falls back to diffuse for every method, and a Burley closure after a diffuse bounce falls back to diffuse, as Cycles does; the random walk keeps its closure. Thin Wall replaces the BSSRDF with Cycles' thin branch (`svm/closure.h`) for every method: no probe or walk, and the diffuse keeps `1 - Subsurface Weight` plus the thin reflection `Subsurface Weight × sat(0.5 (1 - Subsurface Anisotropy))`. Its translucent transmission (`bsdf_thin_subsurface_setup`) is not evaluated, so Thin Wall is reported as `UNSUPPORTED_FEATURE` under `shader.principled.thin_wall`. The Principled's `subsurface_method` node property selects the method (`BURLEY`, `RANDOM_WALK`, `RANDOM_WALK_SKIN`, `RANDOM_WALK_LEGACY`). A Mix or Add of two arms under different methods has no single method and reports `shader.principled.subsurface_method.mixed` with its closure dropped, so Potter never renders one method as another. Subsurface IOR is read by the skin method and Subsurface Anisotropy by the random walk methods and the thin branch. The raster preview and the realtime engine still shade the diffuse lobe without subsurface scattering (a known gap). Verified by `tests/native_subsurface_sphere.rs` against Blender 5.2.2 renders of a sphere under a point and a disk light (`subsurface_sphere.py`, rendered live). At subsurface scale 6 the Burley case differs from no subsurface by 0.040 in red, and the random walk case differs from Burley by 0.119, so an absent or misscaled closure of any method is detected.
- The random walk subsurface refracts its entry at the Principled's Specular IOR Level eta (`kernel/svm/closure.h`, `bssrdf->ior = eta`), with the roughness clamped to `[0, 1]` before it is squared, so a Specular IOR Level other than 0.5 changes the entry as in Blender. Its exit faces along the walk's last flight, as Cycles reverses that ray at the exit (`kernel/integrator/subsurface.h`); a Burley exit keeps its probe's orientation.
- The Burley subsurface exit matches Cycles 5.2.2 in these places that were wrong: the probe's disk axes use Cycles' `make_orthonormals(Ng)` basis (`kernel/integrator/subsurface_disk.h`; the basis changes the exit weights, since the tangent and bitangent probes are not symmetric about the normal); the exit's diffuse closure takes its own normal as its view, as Cycles' diffuse closure has no view term (a far-side exit was zeroed before); a subsurface entry takes no bounce, since `subsurface_bounce` returns before `path_state_next` (`kernel/integrator/shade_surface.h`, `path_state.h`), and the exit keeps the entry's bounce count, so a zero-budget sphere is no longer black; the probe is a local intersection, so no volume transport or light hit applies along it; and a sheen sample sets the diffuse ancestor, as Cycles' `LABEL_DIFFUSE` does (`bsdf_sheen.h`). On the oracle sphere Potter matches Blender 5.2.2 to about 0.5% in each channel.
- Hook, Cast, and Simple Deform follow Blender 5.2.2: Hook origin handling and Cast auto size resolve as Blender does, Simple Deform evaluates Blender's deform methods, and Hook and Warp falloff curves use Blender's curve evaluation, including Y clipping.
- Laplacian Smooth and Corrective Smooth match Blender 5.2.2, including Corrective bind cases that bind a flat mesh and evaluate it after the mesh moves.
- Mesh Deform harmonic binds solve with a pinned Eigen build and match Blender's bind weights, including after subdivision; Laplacian Deform evaluates from its stored bind data.
- Armature modifiers deform vertices along evaluated B-Bone segment curves. Segmented B-Bone constraint target sampling and armature constraints weighted through B-Bone segments still return catalogued `UnsupportedFeature` instead of linear-transform approximations.
- Texture coordinate mapping (Local, Global, Object, UV) is shared by the texture-driven modifiers, and `texture.create` creates the texture resources they reference.
- Image textures honour Blender's ImageUser selection, source alpha, and orientation settings, and EXR multilayer images expose their layers and passes to texture lookups.
- Primitive grids store their edges in Blender's grid order, so subdivision-numbered edge points and bind indices match Blender. Vertex and edge ids are unchanged.
- Skin branch hulls evaluate with Blender's hull, repair, and tagged-edge rules. The branch-hull triangle merge visits edges in Blender's BMesh slot order (freed slots reused last-freed first), so equal-score ties resolve as Blender does.
- Volume Displace samples its texture in index space with Blender's largest voxel size, so affine grids match Blender's index-space displacement, and decoded VDB density is displaced at Blender's sample positions.
- Subdivision keeps original-vertex indices after Simple subdivision and clears them after Catmull-Clark, as Blender does.
- Clip materials export to `.blend` with Blender's glTF MASK alpha stage (`io_scene_gltf2/blender/imp/pbrMetallicRoughness.py` 630-700): a DITHERED material whose Principled Alpha is `SUBTRACT(1, LESS_THAN(x, cutoff))`, where `x` is the texture alpha times the base alpha factor. A material without a texture writes the binary constant instead (1 where the base alpha reaches the cutoff, else 0). Before, the export set Alpha to the base alpha, so Blender drew a dithered transparency and the cutout was lost. `.blend` import reads that stage back as `clip`, with the graph's cutoff as the threshold, when the material has no `potter.alpha_mode` marker; the stage must drive the active output's Surface straight from the Principled BSDF, so a Mix Shader in between is not a clip. glTF export keeps the texture alpha factor as the MASK's `baseColorFactor` alpha, so a textured MASK cuts the same pixels after a round trip. Verified by `tests/native_clip_mask.rs` against Blender 5.2.2 Cycles, on the importer's gradient graph, with a one-pixel box filter on both sides.
- Known gap: Blender's glTF importer leaves no mark that a factor-only MASK was a MASK. It sets Alpha to the binary value and DITHERED, and stores only the glTF `extras` as custom properties (`io_scene_gltf2/blender/imp/material.py` 50), so such a `.blend` material is indistinguishable from an authored DITHERED constant-alpha material and imports as `opaque`. It renders identically (0 hides, 1 shows); only the `clip` label and threshold are lost. Potter's own export marks clip (`potter.alpha_mode`), so Potter round trips are exact.
- Known gap: Potter's Texture Coordinate `Generated` output is the object-space position, while Cycles normalises it to the object's bounds over 0 to 1 (`intern/cycles/blender/mesh.cpp` 682-705 with `mesh_texture_space`, `mesh.cpp` 532-552), so a Generated gradient, noise or checker on a mesh that is not bounded by [0, 1] differs from Blender. The clip oracle uses UV for this reason.
- Realtime Beauty shades flat faces per pixel, as smooth faces and EEVEE's per-fragment shadows are. Flat faces were lit once per triangle at its centre, and realtime split each flat triangle into 16 sub-triangles so that shadows would step finer; a shadow edge across a large flat face therefore followed the sub-triangle grid, and a 20-unit floor (cells of about 5 units) could show no shadow where EEVEE shows one. Every realtime Beauty hit is now lit, shadow included, at its own point with the geometric normal (`render/raster.rs` `hit_color`, `render/scene.rs` `SceneShader`), and the subdivision and the centre bake are gone, so flat faces keep their triangles and the shadow scene is built over them. The albedo pass of a flat face is its base colour, as a smooth face's already was. `tests/fixtures/native_oracle/realtime_transparent_shadow.py` keeps the floor flat, and `tests/realtime_transparent_shadow.rs` compares it with EEVEE 5.2.2 (`realtime_transparent_shadow_spread.json`, re-measured on the flat floor). A pre-change model of the patch, sampling each cell's centroid, gives shadowed-to-lit ratios of 1.000 where the exact ratios are 0.827 and 0.654 (EEVEE: 0.830, 0.655); the per-pixel model matches them.
- Primitive UVs have consumer-visible effects, matching Blender's export defaults. STL stores no UV coordinates, so an STL export of a primitive reports `mesh.uv_layer` (strict export refuses; `--allow-lossy` permits), as Blender's STL writer drops them. PLY writes one vertex per unique (vertex, UV) pair, as Blender's PLY exporter does with `export_uv` on, and its importer keeps those vertices (`merge_verts` off), so a cube round-trips to 14 vertices. OBJ writes `f v/vt/vn` corners when a UV layer exists, as Blender's OBJ writer does. USD writes UV `st` primvars `faceVarying`, as Blender's USD writer does.
- Sculpt dyntopo keeps a mesh's UV layers in step with its faces. Before, a split or collapse changed a face's corner count without touching its UV store, so dyntopo failed with `SCENE_INVALID` on any mesh with a UV layer (now every primitive). Blender 5.2.2 rebuilds the faces around a split edge or a collapsed vertex with default loop data (`pbvh_bmesh.cc`, `BM_face_create` → `bm_loop_create`, default UV `(0, 0)`), and its operator warns that dyntopo does not preserve UVs (`sculpt_dyntopo.cc`). Potter follows that rule: corners of rebuilt faces take `[0, 1]` (Blender's `(0, 0)` with v pointing down), and corners of untouched faces keep their UV. Potter keeps n-gons where Blender triangulates, so the corners of a rebuilt n-gon are an approximation. Enabling dyntopo on a target with UV layers or other corner data reports `sculpt.dyntopo.face_data_lost`. `geom/sculpt.rs` `remap_corner_data`.
- Primitives carry Blender's default `UVMap` layer (`calc_uvs`; the torus always has one), with Blender 5.2.2's per-loop values in its storage order (`geom/primitive_uv.rs`): the cube (`BM_mesh_calc_uvs_cube`), plane and grid (`BM_mesh_calc_uvs_grid`), circle (`BM_mesh_calc_uvs_circle`), cylinder and cone (`BM_mesh_calc_uvs_cone`, with the strip and cap rules), the torus (`add_uvs` in `add_mesh_torus.py`) and the icosphere at every level (the `icouvs` table, carried through `subdivide_edges` by Blender's `BM_edge_split` interpolation), and the UV sphere (`bm_mesh_calc_uvs_sphere_face`). Before, primitives had no UV layer, so exports and textures had no texture coordinates. Potter stores `v` down, so the values are flipped at the boundary. `tests/primitive_uv_layers_oracle.rs` compares the loops with the committed Blender oracle.
- Rough glass follows Cycles 5.2.2's GGX glass closure (`kernel/closure/bsdf_microfacet.h`: `generalized_schlick_setup`, `bsdf_microfacet_eval`, `bsdf_microfacet_sample`, `microfacet_ggx_preserve_energy` on the `ggx_glass_E` tables, and `kernel/svm/closure.h` for the transmission block). The Principled Transmission Weight and the Glass BSDF node share one lobe: a reflection and a transmission lobe with the real dielectric Fresnel remapped from F0 to 1, weighted `(1 - metallic) · transmission`. Principled tints the transmission by the square root of its base colour and Glass by its Color, as Cycles does. Each Principled arm derives its tint from its own textured base colour, so a material-level base colour texture reaches the tint as it reaches the colour. Mix and Add weigh the glass tints by each arm's transmission, as they weigh the coat and sheen. A non-glass closure has no transmission (Diffuse, Glossy, Emission and an empty shader socket take it as zero), so a Diffuse arm no longer refracts a material's transmission value. A material-level base colour texture now multiplies only the Principled arms' base colour, where it used to multiply the whole graph's; a texture graph clears that field, so only a material with both a graph and the field is affected. A back face inverts the IOR. The path tracer draws the lobe with the Heitz visible-normal sampler, and its density, MIS and light sampling match Cycles' eval and pdf on both sides of the surface, including the half vectors the sampler never draws, as Cycles' eval also covers them: a light below a glass surface is light-sampled through the transmission lobe. Roughness below Cycles' `roughness_is_almost_specular` threshold is the sharp limit, drawn as a delta with the Fresnel split, and the sharp limit reflects as well as refracts, where the old delta refracted only. An IOR of 1 refracts straight through as a delta, as `bsdf_microfacet_sample` makes it (`|eta - 1| < 1e-4`), while its eval keeps the roughness-only flag. The Principled tint is no longer `base_color`, and the catalog no longer reports `shader.principled.transmission_ggx`. Shadow rays follow Cycles' shadow transparency: only a Transparent closure's extinction `(1 - alpha) · tint` passes them (`kernel/integrator/surface_shader.h`, `surface_shader_transparency`), so a glass surface blocks a shadow ray, where the old rule passed `transmission · base colour` through it. Compared with Blender 5.2.2 Cycles renders: a glass plane lit by a disk on either side (`tests/fixtures/native_oracle/glass/glass_plane_means.json`, generated by `glass_plane_probe.py`; roughness 0, 0.2 and 0.5 at IOR 1.45 and 1.5, a tinted Principled and a tinted Glass node), and a rough glass sphere under a point and a disk light (`tests/native_glass_sphere.rs`, rendered by `glass_sphere.py`; roughness 0.2, Blender at 256 samples and Potter at 32 on a 16 × 16 frame, eight seeds per side, with a t-quantile tolerance from the seed spread; the plane oracle covers both roughnesses). The unit tests in `render/principled_bsdf.rs` (`glass_tests`) check the density against the accepted draws over the sampler's domain, the table-node albedo against the GGX glass energy, reciprocity, the sharp Fresnel split and the IOR-1 delta. Known gaps: the realtime raster does not evaluate transmission (EEVEE shows transmitted light through the same plane, at values that do not match Cycles either: back-side 26.07 against Cycles' 66.86 at IOR 1.5 and roughness 0, and 62.56 against 67.34 at IOR 1.45), and the Glass node's Distribution is always Multiscatter GGX, Blender's default.
- Plane, grid, circle, cylinder, cone, torus and icosphere primitives store their vertices, edges, faces and loops in Blender 5.2.2's `primitive_*_add` order (`bmo_create_grid_exec`, `bmo_create_circle_exec`, `bmo_create_cone_exec`, `bmesh/intern/bmesh_construct.cc` `BM_edges_from_verts_ensure`). Each face creates its missing edges closing edge first; a circle's ring starts at `(0, r)` and runs counter-clockwise; a cone or cylinder with a zero radius collapses that end to one apex, with its cap fans and N-gon caps as Blender dissolves them. Cylinder and cone vertices now follow Blender's order: the two cap centres first, then each segment's bottom and top vertex, so an element ID that named a vertex under the old order (`v<N>`) names a different one now. Before, the plane was a different vertex order from Blender's grid, circles ran clockwise, and the edges of every primitive followed Potter's own order. `tests/primitive_order_oracle.rs` compares the ordered arrays with the committed Blender oracle `tests/fixtures/primitive_uvs/primitive_uv_oracle.json` (generator `generate_primitive_uv_oracle.py`). The torus stores each edge lower vertex first, in the closing-first walk of its faces, as Blender's torus script does. Blender creates those edges in `mesh_calc_edges`, which from 1000 faces splits them over hash maps chosen by the host's thread count (up to 8), so Blender's order differs between hosts there; Potter matches the single-map order, which Blender gives with `--threads 1` (`tests/fixtures/primitive_uvs/torus_single_map_oracle.json`), and matches Blender's order exactly below 1000 faces. Icosphere edges follow `bmo_subdivide.cc`'s `subdivide_edges` order at every subdivision level: each original edge's slot, then the split halves and cut edges in edge order, then each face's grid last face first (`tests/primitive_order_oracle.rs`, levels 1 to 4).
- The `uv_sphere` primitive (`Mesh::uv_sphere`, `node.create kind uv_sphere`) stores its vertices in Blender 5.2.2's `primitive_uv_sphere_add` order, with positions bit-identical to Blender's f32 output (`geom/uv_sphere.rs`, `bmo_create_uvsphere_exec`, `remove_doubles` over a kd-tree). Edges and faces follow the same extrusion with boundary edges in creation order. - Realtime shadows from transparent surfaces follow EEVEE 5.2.2's transparent shadow pass (`eevee_surf_shadow.bsl.hh`, `eevee_shader.cc` `transparent_shadows`, `eevee_material.cc` `has_transparent_shadows`). A shadow ray that hits a triangle of a material EEVEE compiles as transparent multiplies its light by that surface's pass, the average of `(1 - alpha) · tint` at the hit (`render/path.rs` `hit_params`, `render/scene.rs` `transparent_shadow_pass`). A material is transparent when its graph reaches a Transparent BSDF whose Color is linked or not black, or a Principled whose Alpha is linked or below one (`shader/evaluator.rs` `graph_is_transparent`, stored once as `PreparedMaterial::is_transparent`); a material without a graph is transparent when its alpha mode is not `opaque` and its base alpha is below one. The point and spot shadow maps hold only the triangles that cannot cast a transparent shadow, and a transparent caster's part is one ray to the light. Sun and area lights average the same nine jittered rays as before, each now expecting the product of its transparent passes. The shadow is neutral, as EEVEE's is (`closure_to_rgba_shadow` gives grey alpha). Glass casts an opaque shadow, since refraction is no transparent closure in EEVEE (`closure_eval(ClosureTransparency)` is the only writer of `g_transmittance`). The render method (DITHERED or BLENDED) does not change the shadow: EEVEE reads only the transparency flag and `MA_BL_TRANSPARENT_SHADOW` for it. A clip material passes its binarised alpha, as its visible surface does; the bridge exports a clip material as a Principled Alpha equal to its base alpha (`_set_principled_values`), so EEVEE's pass on that exported file is fractional, and Potter's is 0 or 1 per hit. Materials gain `use_transparent_shadow` (default true, `MA_BL_TRANSPARENT_SHADOW`), read and written through `.blend`, `material.create` and `material.update`, and reported by `inspect`. A `.blend` material with EEVEE's `use_backface_culling_shadow` on imports with the loss `blender.material.backface_culling_shadow`, since Potter's shadow rays hit both sides. Checked by `tests/realtime_transparent_shadow.rs` against EEVEE 5.2.2 renders of a sun and a point light over a flat floor; each EEVEE ratio and tolerance is measured and derived in `tests/fixtures/native_oracle/realtime_transparent_shadow_spread.json`. The point light's transparent tolerance is about 0.034, since Potter traces that occluder exactly, and its EEVEE spread over five realisations rose to 0.0057 on the flat floor; its opaque tolerance is about 0.13, because the 64-texel depth map places an edge to within 1.5 texels, and a 1.5-texel move can flip one pixel of the 81-pixel patch. The sun's tolerances are about 0.008 (transparent) and 0.001 (opaque).

- The Solid preview (`pot preview --mode solid`) lights surfaces as Blender 5.2.2's Workbench STUDIO lighting does with the factory `Default` studio light (`BKE_studiolight_default`; `workbench_world_light.bsl.hh`, `workbench_composite.bsl.hh`, `workbench_prepass.bsl.hh`). Potter's old Solid lighting was a fixed ambient term and two hard-coded directional lights, single-sided: a back face was lit by its geometric normal as if it faced the viewer. Now every pixel is lit in the camera's view space by the four studio lights, with Blender's wrapped diffuse, Blinn specular and brightened Fresnel terms, a back face is lit as its front, and roughness and metallic are quantized as Workbench's material buffer and G-buffer store them (`render/studio.rs`). Flat faces are lit per pixel too, since the specular term depends on the view ray. Verified by `tests/workbench_studio_light.rs` against Blender 5.2.2 Workbench renders of a sphere and a plane (`tests/fixtures/native_oracle/workbench_studio_light.py`) from a perspective and an orthographic camera, and by `blend_solid_preview_shades_with_viewport_display_under_the_scene_view_transform`, which imports a `.blend` whose node tree differs from its viewport display and compares the Standard and AgX views with Workbench. - Solid previews (`pot preview --mode solid`) are display-encoded through the scene's view transform and look, as Beauty is (`compositor::encode_display_output`, which skips the compositor graph). Before, Solid used a fixed sRGB curve, so a scene set to AgX or Filmic previewed its Solid background and surfaces in sRGB, where Blender's Workbench applies the view transform (checked headless with Standard and AgX). The preview manifest reports the scene's view transform for Beauty and Solid, and `sRGB standard` for the data passes; before, it reported `sRGB standard` for every mode. `tests/workbench_studio_light.rs` compares both views against Workbench.
- The Principled sheen lobe follows Cycles 5.2.2's microfiber sheen (`kernel/closure/bsdf_sheen.h`: `bsdf_sheen_setup`, `bsdf_sheen_eval`, `bsdf_sheen_sample`) over the `table_sheen_ltc` LTC table, which `scripts/extract_cycles_principled_tables.py` now extracts. The sheen layer is set up first, so its layering weight `1 - max(sheen weight · tint · albedo)` attenuates the coat, diffuse, specular, metallic and transmission lobes and the emission, as `closure_layering_weight` does (`kernel/svm/closure.h`, `principled_bsdf_emission`). The path tracer draws sheen directions as a branch of the lobe mixture, so its density and power-heuristic MIS stay exact; the raster preview evaluates the sheen lobe towards an area light's centre like the specular lobe. Mix and Add combine Sheen Weight, Sheen Tint and Sheen Roughness with the same rule as the coat. Sheen Roughness defaults to Blender's 0.5 (it was 0.3). Verified against Blender 5.2.2 single-bounce renders with the committed 2.4 disk light (`tests/fixtures/native_oracle/principled/single_bounce_sheen_r12_means.json`): a white sheen at roughness 0.5, a tinted sheen at roughness 0.3, and a roughness-0.5 sheen over a white base with the default specular layer. Sheen is removed from the Principled inputs reported as unsupported.
- A Diffuse BSDF is a Lambert lobe with no specular lobe, as Blender's Diffuse node is (`source/blender/nodes/shader/nodes/node_shader_bsdf_diffuse.cc`). Potter's Diffuse closure kept the default dielectric layer and fed its Roughness input into the GGX lobe, so a roughness-0 Diffuse surface lit by a sun seen along its mirror direction rendered about 1600 times too bright (the GGX lobe is singular at roughness 0). The layer is now off for Diffuse only; Glossy, Glass and Principled are unchanged. Its Roughness input (Oren-Nayar in Blender) is still not evaluated. Found by the tinted-Transparent oracle (`tests/native_graph_transparent.rs`), which renders a Diffuse floor under a unit sun against Blender 5.2.2 Cycles.
- Realtime shadows from a Transparent surface were opaque: the tinted Transparent BSDF over a diffuse floor rendered black in `pot render --engine realtime` (floor-only `0.2546`; EEVEE `[0.0675, 0.0675, 0.0675]`). They follow EEVEE's transparent shadow pass now (see the entry above). The realtime colour of a visible transparent surface is not re-measured in this entry.
- A material's graph is the only source of its transparency, as Cycles reads it. The path tracer and the realtime Beauty raster honour a Transparent BSDF, a Mix Shader with one, and a Principled Alpha under any render method. The path tracer forced alpha 1 for `opaque`, and the raster ignored the alpha of `opaque` surfaces. A `.blend` material whose render method is DITHERED imports as `opaque` (its Principled Alpha is no longer read as a `clip` threshold): Blender 5.2.2 never thresholds alpha, `alpha_threshold` is read only by the 4.2 versioning (`source/blender/blenloader/intern/versioning_420.cc`), and Cycles and EEVEE both dither the graph's transparency. A material without a graph has no transparency of its own, as before. Mixing a Transparent arm no longer dilutes the other arm's colour, reflectance, normal or transmission: a Mix Shader's shading is weighted by each arm's share of the rays that reach it (`shader/evaluator.rs` `BsdfParams::mix`), as Cycles' Transparent closure carries only that share (`intern/cycles/kernel/closure/bsdf_transparent.h`). A DITHERED material's texture alpha no longer multiplies an opaque surface's alpha. `tests/native_graph_transparent.rs` checks a DITHERED Mix(0.5, Transparent, Principled) in front of an emitter against Blender's Cycles and EEVEE renders.
- A graph's closures take their alpha from the graph alone: Diffuse, Glossy, Glass, Emission, and an empty shader socket start at alpha 1, so a viewport colour alpha (`diffuse_color`, a glTF factor) does not make a graph see-through, as Cycles renders it (`shader/evaluator.rs` `closure_params`). Only a Transparent BSDF, a Principled Alpha, and a Mix of them set alpha. A Transparent BSDF's Color is its tint: a camera pass-through multiplies throughput by it, and a shadow ray's transmittance is `(1 - alpha) · tint`, as Cycles weights those closures (`intern/cycles/kernel/closure/bsdf_transparent.h`). Mix weights each arm's transparency by its factor, and Add sums the arms' transparencies, as Cycles sums closure weights; both are carried as `tint = transparency / (1 - alpha)` over the pass chance `1 - alpha` (`tint_for_transparency`). The one deviation: an Add with a fully opaque arm (alpha 1) has no pass chance, so a transparent share beside it is dropped. Potter's single alpha carries both the shading and the pass chance, and Add keeps the larger opacity for shading, as before. A tinted Transparent BSDF exports to glTF as a reported `material.shader_graph.unsupported_input` loss, since glTF alpha is a scalar. The realtime raster does not tint, and EEVEE's dithered result for a tinted Transparent differs from Cycles' (`tests/native_graph_transparent.rs` checks Cycles only). An OPAQUE material's graph keeps the base colour alpha (a glTF `baseColorFactor` alpha) out of its Principled Alpha, so its Alpha input is one (`exchange/material_graph.rs` `write_principled_inputs`). Checked by `tests/native_graph_transparent.rs` against Blender 5.2.2 Cycles renders and an OPAQUE glTF render.
- The Principled coat follows Cycles 5.2.2's coat block (`kernel/svm/closure.h`, `principled_bsdf_emission`). The coat is a GGX dielectric reflection lobe at Coat Roughness squared, with the IOR's real Fresnel and Cycles' multiscatter energy compensation (`ggx_E`, `Fss` of the dielectric). Its layering albedo `weight · F · gain · E` attenuates the diffuse, specular, metallic and transmission lobes and the emission, and Coat Tint applies `mix(1, tint^(1/cos θt), weight)` per channel to the layers below. The path tracer samples the coat as a branch of the lobe mixture, so density and MIS stay exact. Coat Normal drives the coat lobe and tint; unlinked, it is the shading normal. The coat is removed from the Principled inputs reported as unsupported. Verified against Blender 5.2.2 single-bounce renders with the committed 2.4 disk light (`tests/fixtures/native_oracle/principled/single_bounce_coat_r12_means.json`).
- The `box` primitive (`Mesh::box_mesh`, `node.create kind box`) stores its vertices, edges, faces and loops in Blender 5.2.2's `primitive_cube_add` order (`bmo_create_cube_exec`, `bmesh/operators/bmo_primitive.cc`). Corners enumerate x, then y, then z; faces follow Blender's table; each face creates its missing edges closing edge first, as `BM_edges_from_verts_ensure` does. Before, Potter's corner and face order differed from Blender's, so outputs that keep the box's element order differed. The Geometry Nodes Cube node is a separate implementation and is unchanged. `tests/primitive_box_order.rs` compares the ordered arrays with the committed Blender oracle `tests/fixtures/primitive_box/box_order.json` (generator `generate_box_order_oracle.py`).
- Area and disk lights are hit by BSDF rays with the same density as their light samples. The density is measured from the shading vertex, not from the origin that a crossed emitter moves past, so an emitter behind a transparent pass is no longer weighted with the wrong distance. A light a path has crossed is not found again, so the path needs no crossing memory. An emitter crossed by a transparent pass counts against `transparent_max_bounces`, as Cycles' `integrate_light_forward` counts it (`intern/cycles/kernel/integrator/shade_light.h`), and the path ends at the cap after the emission. A Blender area light's `spread` is imported and exported, and `light.create` and `light.update` accept it on area lights. The path tracer confines the emission to the cone as Cycles' `area_light_spread_attenuation` does, and samples the part of the emitter that the cone reaches (`intern/cycles/kernel/light/area.h`, `area_light_eval`, `area_light_spread_clamp_light`), so the spread no longer raises `blender.light.spread`. Realtime and raster shading light an area light over its whole hemisphere, as Blender's EEVEE 5.2.2 does: a headless EEVEE render of a spread 0.3 light and a spread π light gives identical strip means (0.18883, 0.17523, 0.13146), so the spread is not applied in realtime, matching Blender.
- Path tracing lets BSDF-sampled rays hit area lights, and point or spot lights with a radius, weighted against light sampling with multiple importance sampling, as Cycles does (`intern/cycles/kernel/light/light.h`, `lights_intersect`; `integrator/shade_light.h`, `integrate_light_forward`; `light/sample.h`, `light_sample_mis_weight_forward`). Before, these lights were only light-sampled, so a glossy highlight of a small light was noisy. The estimator has the same expectation. A light's `cycles.use_multiple_importance_sampling` (default on) is imported and exported, and `light.create` and `light.update` accept `use_multiple_importance_sampling`. Camera rays still do not see lights. The last vertex of a path takes no BSDF sample, so its light and environment samples are unweighted; before, its environment samples were weighted without a BSDF partner, which darkened them.
- Material volumes follow Cycles' per-channel extinction and albedo closures (`intern/cycles/kernel/svm/closure.h`, `svm_node_closure_volume`, `svm_node_principled_volume`). A Volume Absorption transmits `exp(-(1 - Color) · Density · d)` per channel, where Potter used `Color^(Density · d)`. A Volume Scatter's Color weights its extinction as well as its scattering (σt = Color · Density, albedo 1), where Potter used grey extinction `Density` with albedo Color. A Principled Volume's Absorption Color is evaluated: absorption `(1 - Color) · (1 - sqrt(Absorption Color)) · Density` per channel, with scatter `Color · Density`. The volume coefficients are absolute per channel (σs, σa, each closure's weight times its Density), so Add Shader sums each closure's extinction and Mix Shader weights it by its factor; Potter used to sum or mix the weights and the densities separately. A surface's Add or Mix no longer sums the material's base volume. Volume Absorption and Volume Scatter default to Color 0.8, Blender's node default (Potter used 0.5). A simple material's volume colour is the Principled Volume colour, as the `.blend` bridge exports it. Verified by `tests/volume_extinction.rs` against Blender 5.2.2 (`tests/fixtures/volume_extinction`, generated by `generate_volume_extinction_oracle.py`): absorption, the default colour, Add Shader, Mix Shader and Principled absorption match Blender within 0.01, and a Volume Scatter conserves a white environment. A voxel Volume with non-grey extinction reports `shader.volume.spectral_extinction`. Two differences remain. A Principled Volume with scattering Color is single-scatter in Potter, so it does not reproduce Blender's multiple scattering. A voxel Volume transports the channel-mean extinction with a per-channel albedo, so its colour is approximated.
- Transparent surfaces follow Cycles' `transparent_max_bounces` (`intern/cycles/kernel/integrator/path_state.h`, `path_state_next`; `intersect_filter.h`; `intersect_shadow.h`). A pass-through no longer consumes the regular `max_bounces`; it counts against `transparent_max_bounces` (at least one, as Cycles' FIXME). The pass that reaches the cap ends the path at the next surface after that surface's emission, with no direct light. A shadow ray crosses at most `transparent_max_bounces` minus the path's passes transparent surfaces and is blocked past that, where Potter allowed a fixed 16 layers. Volume-bounds crossings count against their own limit of 1024 (`VOLUME_BOUNDS_MAX`), not the regular bounces. A camera segment's opacity accumulates through volume transmittance instead of being reset by a later pass-through. Scenes with fewer transparent surfaces in a view ray than the cap render unchanged; deeper stacks show more of the environment, as Cycles does. `cycles.transparent_max_bounces` imports from and exports to `.blend` files; the scene schema gains an optional field with Blender's default of 8 (no schema bump), omitted from the scene JSON when default. Checked by `tests/native_transparent_bounce.rs` (six planes under the default cap) and `tests/native_transparent_cap.rs` (cap and shadow budget, Blender 5.2.2 oracles).
- Realtime Beauty (`pot render --engine realtime`) adds a material's emission (Principled emission colour times strength) to flat faces. Flat faces were baked without it, and smooth triangles of a non-graph material dropped it too, while graph materials kept theirs. Emission is now added once, in `render/scene.rs` `shade` for Beauty, after the reflectance weight and the clamp, as Cycles' `EmissionNode` emits it (`intern/cycles/scene/shader_nodes.cpp`). Strength is not clamped, as in EEVEE (`gpu_shader_material_principled.glsl:179`) and Cycles (`intern/cycles/kernel/svm/closure.h:206-207`); the path tracer's emission term (`render/path.rs`) drops its `max(0.0)` on strength for the same reason, so negative strength subtracts in both engines. `tests/realtime_flat_emission.rs` checks flat, smooth and graph-per-pixel emitter planes under a black world against a Blender 5.2.2 EEVEE render, within 1e-3 linear.
- New materials are double-sided, as Blender 5.2.2's `use_backface_culling` is off by default (`MA_BL_CULL_BACKFACE` unset in `DNA_material_types.h`). `material.create`, `Material::default`, faces with no material (Blender's Default Surface is not culled), and the FBX, USD, OBJ, SVG, and `.blend` importers without a source flag follow it. glTF and VRM keep the file's `doubleSided`, absent meaning single-sided as glTF defines. FBX import reads the `potter:double_sided` property it writes, so a single-sided FBX round trip keeps its value. OBJ export reports `material.mtl.double_sided` for a single-sided material, as MTL has no two-sided flag. No scene migration: `double_sided` is always serialized, and schema 1 documents carry it explicitly.
- Back faces are drawn as Blender draws them. The Solid and Wire previews and the id, normal and depth passes draw the Workbench geometry with back faces (`View3DShading.show_backface_culling` off), where Potter culled them by material `double_sided`, so a single-sided plane seen from behind, or the back triangle of a bowtie, came out empty. Realtime Beauty still culls single-sided material faces seen from behind, as EEVEE does (`use_backface_culling`); the path tracer draws both sides, as Cycles does. A `.blend` whose scene has Workbench backface culling on imports with the loss `blender.scene.display_backface_culling`, since Potter's preview does not cull. Verified by `tests/backface_culling.rs` against Blender 5.2.2 renders of a plane seen from above and below in Workbench (`tests/fixtures/native_oracle/backface_coverage.py`), and of the realtime Beauty from behind in EEVEE.
- `sculpt.stroke` Clay Strips, Inflate, and Layer follow Blender 5.2.2 dab by dab. Each sample is one DOTS dab, applied in order to the positions that earlier dabs left (`geom/sculpt_dabs.rs`, from `sculpt_paint/mesh/brushes/clay_strips.cc`, `inflate.cc`, `layer.cc`, and `sculpt_paint/mesh/sculpt.cc`). Clay Strips builds its strip plane from the area normal and center around each dab, with the 0.15 plane offset, the 0.15 tip roundness, and the 1.2 area radius factor. Its falloff defaults to `smoother`, the new falloff variant (Blender's `BRUSH_CURVE_SMOOTHER`); the other dab brushes default to `smooth`. Mirror symmetry runs each dab once per pass in Blender's order, with the location and grab delta flipped, and scales every pass by Blender's symmetry feather (`calc_symmetry_feather`, on by default in Blender). A mirror stroke therefore no longer errors for these brushes. Verified against Blender 5.2.2 oracles in `tests/fixtures/sculpt_brushes/` (generated by `generate_oracle.py` in a session with a display, because background Blender does not refresh the view that Clay Strips reads), checked by `dab_brushes_match_blender_5_2_2` at 5e-6. Not represented: radial symmetry, and Layer's persistent base across strokes (Layer uses the stroke-start positions as its base).
- `sculpt.stroke` Draw, Draw Sharp, Blob, Nudge, Crease, Pinch, Flatten, Fill, Scrape, Grab, Snake Hook, Thumb, Rotate (Twist), and Elastic Grab follow Blender 5.2.2 dab by dab, on the same framework as Clay Strips, Inflate, and Layer (`geom/sculpt_dabs.rs` and its `sculpt_dabs/` family modules, from `sculpt_paint/mesh/brushes/*.cc` and `sculpt.cc`). Each dab's strength takes the brush's direction flip (`brush_strength`, `brush_flip`), its area normal and plane come from the dab (`calc_area_normal_and_center`, `calc_brush_plane`), and the asset's radii, accumulate flag, and falloff apply, replacing the per-vertex rules. Draw Sharp defaults to the new `pow4` falloff (`brush.cc` POW4). Grab, Thumb, Elastic Grab, and Twist measure each dab from the stroke's first dab and restore the mesh before each dab. Twist no longer takes `delta`, since Blender's angle comes from the stroke's dial. `sculpt.stroke` takes an optional `view` (a unit vector toward the viewer in mesh space) for Blender's facing groups of the area normal and the grab plane; without it, Potter has no view. `elastic_deform` rejects `falloff`, which Blender's Elastic Grab does not use. Twist's dial reads the view when present. `view` is rejected for Pose Lite and Boundary Lite, which keep the per-vertex rules until they follow Blender.
- `sculpt.stroke` Smooth follows Blender 5.2.2 `brushes/smooth.cc` (Laplacian deform type). Each sample is one DOTS dab, and each symmetry pass is one `do_smooth_brush` call. The strength is squared and clamped to `[0, 1]`, then split into up to four iterations (`iteration_strengths`). The first call of a stroke reads positions from its start for every iteration and accumulates the translations; later calls read what the previous iteration wrote. A pass with no vertex in range does nothing and does not count as the first call.
- Vertex normals are one implementation, `geom/normals.rs` `vertex_normals`, Blender's `normals_calc_verts`: each face's Newell normal, weighted by the corner angle at each vertex and then normalized, with a vertex that has no face taking the direction of its position. Before, the sculpt brushes summed the unnormalized cross product of each face's first three corners, and the Displace modifier had its own copy, which gave a loose vertex a zero normal. Sculpt brushes that read vertex normals now receive the new normal, and Displace's loose vertices move along their position direction.
- Custom normals follow Blender 5.2.2 per modifier. A Subdivision Surface with Use Custom Normals on writes a custom-normal layer with the evaluated loop normals, even with no input layer, no sharp edges, and at level 1 (before, level 1 wrote none and mixed sharp edges were not reflected). Multires with Use Custom Normals on writes a layer only when the input has one; sharp edges alone do not add it. Verified by a headless Blender 5.2.2 probe (`tests/fixtures/custom_normal_presence`). Adaptive subdivision takes the Subdivision rule.
- Breaking: the `seed` parameter is removed from `sculpt.stroke`, `sculpt.dyntopo`, `paint.vertex`, `paint.weight` and `paint.texture`. Blender 5.2.2's sculpt brushes have no random factor (brush jitter moves the dab and defaults to 0), and the paint ops never read the value, so the parameter was ignored. Operation batches that still send `seed` are rejected, and stored history loads drop the key with an `operation.seed_retired` warning. Draw displacement has no per-vertex random factor. ClayStrips, Inflate and Layer no longer apply a per-vertex random factor in the `[0.98, 1.0]` range, and they now follow Blender's dab rules (the `sculpt.stroke` bullet above).
- The sculpt `draw` brush displaces by Blender 5.2.2's Draw rule: `normal × radius × strength² × pressure × falloff` (`draw.cc:194-197`, `sculpt.cc:2338-2360`). The earlier rule was `normal × strength × pressure × falloff` with no radius factor and no squaring. `radius` is the world-space radius, and `strength` keeps its meaning. Draw displacement is therefore `radius × strength` times what it was. Other brushes are unchanged. A Blender oracle (`tests/fixtures/multires/sculpt_oracle`) pins the driven-armature stroke in `tests/sculpt_paint.rs`.
- `pot preview` of a subdivided mesh no longer tests every triangle for every pixel. `render/raster.rs` `nearest_visible_hit` and `composite_sample` scanned all prepared triangles per pixel (and per alpha sub-sample), so a 768 px preview cost about pixels × triangles; a level-5 subdivided cube took about 30 to 40 s. The visibility BVH in `render/bvh.rs` now builds over arbitrary primitive bounds and returns each ray's candidate triangles. The nearest hit keeps the exact least (depth, index) rule, and composite layers keep their triangle order, so the output does not change. Measured on this machine with release builds, one run per case, 768 px, `pot preview` wall time, baseline origin/main 9c47cfca → head 24cb8c86: a box of size 2 at subdivision level 5, solid 31.76 s → 1.33 s, wire 35.63 s → 1.27 s, normal 33.75 s → 0.51 s, depth 31.26 s → 0.44 s, id 44.14 s → 0.34 s; a 16×16-subdivision grid at level 2, solid 40.37 s → 0.24 s, wire 50.01 s → 0.47 s, normal 43.08 s → 0.12 s, depth 41.00 s → 0.54 s, id 49.07 s → 0.15 s. The PNG output is byte-identical to the baseline in all ten cases (`cmp`). The unit test `bounded_nearest_hit_matches_a_scan_of_every_triangle` checks every pixel of a tied-depth grid against a full scan.
- `.blend` UDIM tiles keep Blender's tile labels, and a TILED image with no loadable tile exports as a TILED placeholder with its missing base and tiles reported as losses (before, the export failed). A glTF or FBX export of a UDIM image reports `material.texture_udim`; with `--allow-lossy` it writes tile 1001 only (Blender's glTF exporter splits geometry per tile, which Potter does not). An edit to a tile of a file image whose base is missing stores the tile and keeps the base missing (before, it failed). A UDIM image's export sends its tiles only, reading its PNG once.
- `.blend` import and export carry UDIM tiles. A TILED Blender image imports every tile, not only its base: each tile is an immutable blob keyed by its tile number (1002 and up, beside the 1001 base), read from its packed bytes or from the file that its `<UDIM>` or `<UVTILE>` token names. The image keeps a `tiled` flag, so a TILED image with only its base tile still routes its lookups as Blender does. A tile or base whose file is missing stays in the image record with no blob (`tiles[].blob` is `null`, a missing base has no size), and is reported as a `blender.image.udim_tile_missing` loss. Export writes a Potter UDIM image as one packed TILED Blender image with all of its listed tiles at their numbers: an image whose tiles are all 8-bit PNG packs each tile's original bytes, and any other image packs float EXR with the record's alpha mode. A missing tile or base is not packed and is reported as an export loss. Before, a TILED image imported as a single tile, so tiles 1002 and up were lost.
- Image Texture lookups follow Cycles at exact and near-edge UVs, for every extension and UDIM tile. A closest lookup takes `frac` of the Blender coordinate scaled by the image size with no offset, then the extension (`intern/cycles/kernel/device/cpu/image.h`, `interp_closest`): the floor for every coordinate except an exact negative integer, which Cycles reads one texel lower. Potter flipped V before flooring, so an exact integer V read the neighbouring row. A UDIM tile is routed by truncating the coordinate (`kernel/util/image_2d.h`, `kernel_image_udim_map`), so a coordinate in (-1, 0) stays in tile column or row 0, and a missing tile is Cycles' missing colour, magenta (`IMAGE_MISSING_RGBA`), where Potter returned transparent black. Each tile is sampled as its own image at its local coordinate, with the same rule as a single image. Clip returns transparent per texel, so a linear sample partly inside the image keeps its inside weight, and a closest sample on the far edge is transparent. Linear sampling of an in-image coordinate is unchanged. Verified by `tests/fixtures/repeat_edge`: 8 single-image cases (REPEAT, EXTEND, CLIP, MIRROR, Closest and Linear, 16 x 16 pixel-centre UVs in eighths from -0.75 to 1.125) and 8 UDIM cases (tiles 1001 and 1002, the tile-1002 boundary at exactly u = 1.0, and a missing tile 1003), checked by `edge_oracles_match_blenders_render` to 1e-6.
- World and environment reads use the same sampler rule, with REPEAT and no Alpha link, as Cycles' environment texture does (`scene/shader_nodes.cpp`, `EnvironmentTextureNode::image_params`). The equirectangular u was mirrored: Potter used `u = atan2(y, x) / 2π + 0.5`, where Cycles' `direction_to_equirectangular` gives `u = 0.5 − atan2(y, x) / 2π` (`intern/cycles/kernel/camera/projection.h`), so every environment was flipped left to right. The world reader and the environment importance map now use Cycles' mapping. Verified by `tests/fixtures/repeat_edge/blender_world_env_*` (256 directions around the u = 0 seam and up to the zenith, Closest and Linear, Blender 5.2.2 renders), checked by `environment_samples_match_blenders_render` at 1e-6. An environment seam now wraps instead of clamping. The vertex-weight mask and intensity readers keep their own rule.
- The equirectangular panorama camera's basis is confirmed against Blender's panorama camera source (`intern/cycles/kernel/camera/projection.h`, `intern/cycles/util/projection.h`, `intern/cycles/blender/camera.cpp`), a refactor with no output change.
